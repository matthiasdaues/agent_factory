# Report Policy

## Purpose

The report policy governs how research findings are presented in the final report. It ensures that only surviving claims support factual statements, that qualifications and limitations remain visible, and that language does not overstate what the evidence can support.

## Requirements

### Factual Basis

Every factual statement in the report must rest on one or more surviving claims from the frozen claim register. Statements unsupported by surviving claims are not permitted.

### Claim Citations

Every factual statement must cite the ID of at least one surviving claim that supports it. The mapping between statement and claim ID must be precise and verifiable.

### Preservation of Material Qualifications

All material qualifications recorded in the supporting claim must appear in the report. A qualification is material if its removal would mislead the reader about the scope or certainty of the finding. Examples include:

- scope boundaries ("applies only to populations in the northern hemisphere"),
- temporal limitations ("current as of June 2025"),
- uncertainty ranges ("approximately 15%, with a margin of error of ±3%"),
- excluded conditions ("does not apply to emergency medical situations").

### Visibility of Failed or Inconclusive Tests

Important tests that the surviving claim failed or returned inconclusive results must remain visible to the reader. At minimum, the report must:

- identify the key test that could have refuted the claim,
- state what the test looked for and what result would count against the claim,
- describe the actual outcome,
- explain why the claim survived despite the uncertain or negative result.

Tests must not be hidden, minimized, or omitted simply because they complicate the narrative.

### Distinction Between Findings and Recommendations

Findings (what the evidence supports) must be clearly separated from recommendations (what ought to be done as a result). A recommendation is not a finding and may not rest on surviving claims alone; it requires additional value judgments outside the scope of evidence.

### Identification of Unresolved Questions

Where evidence identifies a question that the research cannot answer, the report must state it explicitly. Do not omit unanswered questions to create a false appearance of completeness.

### Avoidance of Proof Language

The report must not use language that presents a surviving claim as proved, certain, or true. A surviving claim has only survived the defined tests within its stated scope; it has not been proved true in an absolute sense.

## Preferred Wording

When describing the status of a surviving claim, use one of these formulations:

- "survived the defined tests,"
- "not refuted within the tested scope,"
- "provisionally retained,"
- "remains open to refutation."

These phrases make clear that survival is conditional—resting on the completion of defined tests and remaining vulnerable to new evidence.

## Prohibited Wording

Do not use:

- "is true,"
- "is proved,"
- "is certain,"
- "is fact,"
- "cannot be challenged,"
- "is beyond doubt."

Such language misrepresents the epistemological status of a surviving claim.

## Application

These requirements apply during report generation and validation. A report that violates them fails semantic review and must not be released.

## Not Covered by This Policy

This policy does not decide:

- which claims deserve prominence in the report,
- how to present statistical uncertainty,
- how to weigh competing findings,
- the appropriate level of detail for an audience.

Those decisions rest with the report writer, guided by domain knowledge and audience needs.
