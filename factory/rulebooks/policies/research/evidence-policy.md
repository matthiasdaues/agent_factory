# Evidence Policy

## Purpose

The evidence policy governs how evidence is recorded, referenced, and evaluated in research artifacts. It ensures that evidence is verifiable, independent, and precisely documented, distinguishing between raw evidence and interpretation.

## Requirements

All evidence supporting a claim must satisfy the following requirements:

### Source Reference

Every piece of evidence must include a precise source reference sufficient to locate and verify the exact passage, statement, or data cited. Vague references ("as discussed in the paper" without page numbers or quotation) are insufficient.

### Source Date

The date of publication, issuance, or creation of the source must be recorded. For sources with multiple relevant dates (publication date, event date, update date), all material dates must be captured.

### Source Provenance

The origin and chain of custody of the source must be clear. Record:

- the original author or issuing body,
- the publisher or distributor,
- whether the source is primary (original observation, firsthand account) or secondary (commentary, summary, interpretation),
- any known institutional affiliation or potential bias of the author.

### Source-Family Identification

Each source must be assigned to a source family. A source family comprises all copies, editions, reprints, or translations of a single original work. Several copies of the same source, even if found independently, belong to a single source family.

**Principle: Repetition is not independent corroboration.** Finding the same source twice does not count as finding two independent sources. Only one source family may be credited per finding, even if multiple copies were discovered.

### Source Limitations

The known scope, accuracy, and limitations of the source must be recorded, including:

- the population or subject it covers,
- the time period it spans,
- the methodology used (if applicable),
- known measurement uncertainty or error margins,
- any conditions under which the source may be unreliable.

### Contrary-Evidence Searches

Evidence is incomplete without a record of what was searched for but not found. For each material claim, evidence must include a documented search for contrary evidence. Record:

- the search strategy and keywords used,
- where the search was conducted (which databases, archives, sources),
- the date of the search,
- the result (what was found or not found).

Absence of search documentation suggests the contrary-evidence searches were not undertaken.

### Separation of Evidence and Interpretation

Raw evidence (quotations, data, observations, measurements) must be kept distinct from interpretation, inference, and reasoning. The raw material must be preserved as stated in the source. Inferences drawn from that evidence belong in a separate section labeled as such.

Example: "The report states that sales rose 15% (raw evidence). This could indicate growing market demand (interpretation)."

## Application

These requirements apply at the point of evidence recording, before any claim is formulated. Evidence that does not meet these requirements must not be used to support a claim in review or report.

## Not Covered by This Policy

This policy does not decide:

- whether evidence is *true*,
- whether a source is *credible*,
- whether an interpretation is *correct*,
- whether sources are *independent* (that is a semantic review judgment).

Those decisions belong to semantic review and the claim review process.
