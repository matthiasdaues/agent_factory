# With a projection horizon above 1, a write leaves the later PENDING Plans outdated

**What is wrong:** The rebuild changes only the Plan of the next Odate N
(`architecture.dsl:65`). When the first day or the retirement Odate F lies
after N, the rebuild locks the `PLAN` row of N with `SELECT ... FOR SHARE` and
rebuilds nothing. That is correct for a projection horizon of 1, because then
no Plan after N is stored yet. The boundary tick opens N and then projects the
Plan after N, after the write has committed (view `LaterFirstDayAtBoundary`).

A Domain can set a larger horizon. The feature says the boundary tick
materializes `PENDING` Plans "up to the configured projection horizon"
(PLN-02), and `domain_registry/validators.py` accepts a horizon from 0 to 366.
Take a Domain with horizon 3 on Odate 10-10. The `PENDING` Plans of 10-11,
10-12 and 10-13 hold Instances of version w of Task `load`:

1. An administrator publishes version y of `load` with first day 10-12.
2. The rebuild locks the `PLAN` row of 10-11 shared and rebuilds nothing.
3. The Plans of 10-12 and 10-13 keep their Instances of w.

The model does not say that a boundary tick writes stored `PENDING` Plans
again. The HorizonResolver "projects into PENDING Plans within the projection
horizon" and "completes a Plan row that a rebuild inserted". If the tick only
writes the Odate that enters the horizon, the Plan of 10-12 opens with the
Instance of w. The gate "Task version runs on the Instance's Odate" (GTE-02)
holds it back with `VERSION_NOT_IN_USE`, and `load` does not run on 10-12.
This is the harm of ATAM-0007, without any race. An activation has the same
gap: the Plans after N keep no Instance of the Task.

The feature Scenario "Task publication triggers re-projection" expects more.
When the publication commits, the HorizonResolver re-evaluates the `PENDING`
Plans that the published Task affects, not only the next one.

**Fix:** Decide which transaction brings the stored Plans after N up to date,
and state it in the HorizonResolver and PlanLifecycleManager descriptions:

- A. Every boundary tick writes every stored `PENDING` Plan within the
  horizon again, each in its own transaction, after the opening of N commits.
  The shared lock on the row of N then covers every later Plan, because each
  is written after the opening. A forecast of a later Plan shows the old
  version until the next tick. The feature Scenario must say so.
- B. The rebuild writes every stored `PENDING` Plan from N to the end of the
  horizon, in ascending Odate order, in the transaction of the write. Every
  Plan is current at commit. A write then holds up to one `PLAN` row per Odate
  of the horizon. That breaks the rule that no transaction holds two `PLAN`
  rows, and a Folder publication with many members rebuilds several Plans in
  one transaction.
- C. Limit the horizon to 1 in Increment 01, because rolling-horizon
  projection is out of scope (`scope-map.md`), and choose A or B with that
  work.

**Decision for the stakeholder:**

1. Case: horizon 3, on Odate 10-10 an administrator publishes version y of
   `load` with first day 10-12.
2. Option A: the Plan of 10-12 shows w until the tick of 10-11 writes it
   again with y, and it opens with y. Option B: the publication rewrites the
   Plans of 10-12 and 10-13 with y before it commits, and holds three `PLAN`
   rows while it does. Option C: the Domain cannot store horizon 3 in
   Increment 01.
3. Recommendation: A, because it keeps one `PLAN` row per transaction and
   keeps writes short. The forecast lags by at most one Odate.
4. The answer changes the HorizonResolver and PlanLifecycleManager
   descriptions, the view `LaterFirstDayAtBoundary`, the Consequences of ADR
   017, and the PLN-02 Scenarios of the feature (option A) or the horizon
   validation (option C).
