## Evidence

- Section 1.1: "Unclear and inconsistent representation of the retracted status of published works may ultimately contribute to continued uncritical use of these invalidated publications in the scholarly literature."
- Section 1.1: "These practices may include using the same DOI for both a retraction notice and the retracted publication; removing the metadata record rather than updating it; and variable display across and within platforms and publication venues".
- Section 1.1: "Other post-publication notices, such as errata, corrigenda, addenda, and corrections, as well as author name changes implemented with or without a linked correction notice, are not within the scope of this Recommended Practice."
- Section 4: "The metadata of the retracted publication has been modified to include “RETRACTED:” in the article title."
- Section 5 summary: "These recommendations include 23 metadata elements for retracted publications (eight Essential, 14 Essential if Available, and one Recommended; see 5.1), and 20 metadata fields for retraction elements".
- Shows: how a retraction is recorded in metadata and passed on to every platform that shows the item. Does not show: any equivalent practice for news corrections.
