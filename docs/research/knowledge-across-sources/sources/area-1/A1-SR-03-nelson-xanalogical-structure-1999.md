## Evidence

- SUMMARY: Xanadu proposes "an entire form of literature where links do not break as versions change; where documents may be closely compared side by side and closely annotated; where it is possible to see the origins of every quotation".
- SUMMARY, criticism of the Web link: "The Web trivialized this original Xanadu model, vastly but incorrectly simplifying these problems to a world of fragile ever-breaking one-way links, with no recognition of change or copyright, and no support for multiple versions or principled re-use."
- SUMMARY: "Serious electronic literature (for scholarship, detailed controversy and detailed collaboration) must support bidirectional and profuse links, which cannot be embedded".
- SUMMARY, two kinds of connection: "survivable deep linkage (content links) and recognizable, visible re-use (transclusion)."
- Same section: "This may be simplified to: connections between things which are *different*, and connections between things which are *the same*."
- Same section: "*a system of visible, principled re-use*, showing the origins and context of quotations, excerpts and anthologized materials, and content transiting betwen versions (transclusions)." (spelling "betwen" as in source)
- Web paragraph: "Its one-way breaking links glorified and fetishized as". The next word, websites, is set in quotation marks in the source. The sentence goes on: "those very hierarchical directories from which we sought to free users, and discarded the ideas of stable publishing, annotation, two-way connection and trackable change."
- Design list: "xu60-- 1960 preliminary design (currently undocumented)" and "transclusion, xu60".

What the source shows: Nelson's link model keeps links outside the documents, makes them two-way, and treats re-use (transclusion) as a separate connection that keeps the origin of quoted content visible. It states his criticism of the Web's embedded one-way link.

What it does not show: that the model was deployed at scale, or any independent measure of link breakage on the Web. The 1960 date for transclusion is the author's own claim; the 1960 design is "currently undocumented".
