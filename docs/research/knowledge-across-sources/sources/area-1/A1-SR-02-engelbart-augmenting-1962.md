## Evidence

- 3a5f: Engelbart introduces the Bush passage as so "well put that I quote it in its entirety".
- 3b6p, typed links: "You can designate as many different kinds of links as you wish, so that you can specify different display or manipulative treatment for the different types."
- 3b6q, link direction: "He said that most links possessed a direction, i.e., they were like an arrow pointing from one substructure to another, so that in setting up a link he must specify the two substructures in a given order."
- 3b6u, links as a record of the basis of a claim: "Let us use what we call 'antecedent links' to point to these, and I'll give you a basic idea of how we structure an argument so that we can quickly track down the essential basis upon which a given statement rests."
- 3b6w: "Each primary antecedent can similarly be linked to its primary antecedents, and so on, until you arrive at the statements representing the premises, the accepted facts, and the objectives upon which this argument had been established."

What the source shows: links between parts of statements, link types, and a direction on most links. Antecedent links trace a statement back to its premises, which is a form of provenance inside one argument structure.

What it does not show: links across separate owners or systems, or any record of who made a link.
