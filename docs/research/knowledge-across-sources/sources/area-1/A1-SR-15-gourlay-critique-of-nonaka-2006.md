## Evidence

- Abstract: "Nonaka's proposition that knowledge is created through the interaction of tacit and explicit knowledge involving four modes of knowledge conversion is flawed."
- Abstract: "Three of the modes appear plausible but none are supported by evidence that cannot be explained more simply."
- Abstract: "The conceptual framework omits inherently tacit knowledge, and uses a radically subjective definition of knowledge: knowledge is in effect created by managers."
- Abstract: "Following Dewey, non‐reflectional behaviour is distinguished from reflective behaviour, the former being associated with tacit knowledge, and the latter with explicit knowledge."

What the source shows: a published criticism of SECI on two grounds, the evidence for the conversion modes and the definition of knowledge, with a replacement framework.
