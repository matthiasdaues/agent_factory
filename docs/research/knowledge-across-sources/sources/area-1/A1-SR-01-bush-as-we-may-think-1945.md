## Evidence

- Section 6, on library indexing: "Our ineptitude in getting at the record is largely caused by the artificiality of systems of indexing."
- Section 6: "Selection by association, rather than by indexing, may yet be mechanized."
- Section 7, the link primitive: "associative indexing, the basic idea of which is a provision whereby any item may be caused at will to select immediately and automatically another. This is the essential feature of the memex. The process of tying two items together is the important thing."
- Section 7, how a link is stored: "The user taps a single key, and the items are permanently joined." and "on each item these dots by their positions designate the index number of the other item."
- Section 7, link reuse: "any item can be joined into numerous trails."
- Section 8, sharing a trail: the user "photographs the whole trail out, and passes it to his friend for insertion in his own memex, there to be linked into the more general trail."
- Section 8: "There is a new profession of trail blazers, those who find delight in the task of establishing useful trails through the enormous mass of the common record."

What the source shows: the memex link is written on both items, so it can be followed from either side. A trail is a named sequence of links. A trail can be copied to another person's memex.

What it does not show: any record of who made a link or when. It names no way to tell which source an item came from beyond the item itself. It shows no implementation.
