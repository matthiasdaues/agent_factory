## Evidence

- Abstract: "We report an ongoing study of how people organize their computer files/folders on the hard disk of their office workstations".
- Abstract, method: "Data on file and folder names and file structure were extracted from the hard disk using a computer program STG FolderPrint Plus, DOS command and screen capture."
- On Malone: "Using interviews and guided office tours, Malone ( 1983 ) observed two kinds of desk organization, neat and messy."
- On Malone: "He also observed that files and piles, have two functions: finding and reminding."
- On Malone: "Malone urged the designers of computer environments to take into account this later function as it was at the time of his writing completely overlooked."
- Own result: "The computer desktop appeared to serve the function of Malone's ( 1983 ) piles, reminding the user of tasks to be completed or needing attention."

What the source shows: personal information management research studies how people keep, name, and re-find their own items, mostly through folders and placement. Malone's finding-and-reminding distinction reaches this study as a summary.

What it does not show: any treatment of links between items or of where an item came from. Provenance is not a topic of the study.
