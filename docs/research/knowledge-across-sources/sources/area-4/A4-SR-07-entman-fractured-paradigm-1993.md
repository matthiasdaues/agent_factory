## Evidence

- p. 51: "The idea of “framing” offers a case study of just the kind of scattered conceptualization I have identified. Despite its omnipresence across the social sciences and humanities, nowhere is there a general statement of framing theory that shows exactly how frames become embedded within and make themselves manifest in a text, or how framing influences thinking."
- p. 51-52: "A literature review suggests that framing is often defined casually, with much left to an assumed tacit understanding of reader and researcher."
- p. 52: "Framing essentially involves selection and salience. To frame is to select some aspects of a perceived reality and make them more salient in a communicating text, in such a way as to promote a particular problem definition, causal interpretation, moral evaluation, and/or treatment recommendation for the item described."
- p. 52: "Typically frames diagnose, evaluate, and prescribe, a point explored most thoroughly by Gamson (1992)."
- p. 57: "The major task of determining textual meaning should be to identify and describe frames".
- Shows: the second formulation of frame: a property of a text, defined by selection and salience and four functions. The criticism that drove it is the casual, scattered use of the term before 1993.
- Does not show: a coding procedure, or evidence that the four functions can be coded reliably.
