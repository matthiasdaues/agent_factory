## Skeptical review of the specifications

**Is the problem real?** Counter-ids solves a real but rare problem: the record shows one collision, ST-0159 to ST-0162. Finding-classification is half real. The six scales and the circular definition of Major exist. The run-to-run variance was never measured before the build, and the old rules then agreed on 20 of 20.

**What if we do nothing?** People renumber at merge, and the manual-mode flow stays blocked. Reviews keep looping on borderline findings, but the rule "only major blocks" removes most of that.

**Could 10 to 30 lines solve it?** Yes for the core of both. A claim is a fetch, an increment, a lease push, and a retry. Severity is three yes-or-no answers. Today `next-id` has 1001 lines and `finding-lint` has 1679.

**What should go?**

- Delete the local counter and `--move-to-origin`. DEC-20 rejected a local fallback, and `--plan-seed` plus `--seed` can do the move.
- Delete the `Counter` port, because one adapter remains.
- Reject `guard-human-fields` outside Claude Code. It has 593 lines and four major defects, and a hidden field name still passes.
- Delete the git check of `in_use`. Both `major` and `critical` block, so it decides only whether the human gets a message.
- Simplify the stop to a blocking finding plus that message.
- Delete the per-finding register files. Minor findings return to the report.
- Delete `type`, because no rule computes anything from it.

**Is the specification larger than the problem?** Yes. Four severity values took 153 Scenarios and a 274 KB QA strategy. The counter fitting has more Scenarios than the counter.

**What is the simplest alternative?** Build one `next-id` of about 100 lines that only claims on `origin`. Add one rulebook page with the three questions and the four-value scale, and one lint of about 50 lines.

The review goes against two stakeholder decisions: the guard in every CLI (DEC-51) and the local counter (DEC-05 of counter-ids-fitting). It does not reopen the rejected random suffix (DEC-01 of counter-ids). The line estimates in the last answer are judgment. Nobody built those versions.

## ✅ Went Well

- **A race test settled the counter design before any spec existed.** `poc/counters/ref-claim-race.sh` handed out 40 IDs on each of three remotes with no failed claim and no duplicate, so the countapi service went away (DEC-12 to DEC-14 of [counter-ids](../proposals/counter-ids.md)). Evidence: the handoff of 2026-11-04, `handoff-counter-ids-git-ref-2026-11-04.md`, kept outside the repository.
- **A short experiment removed a wrong register design before code existed.** A `git merge-file --union` test showed that a deleted last line returns when another branch appends. Evidence: the architecture handoff, `handoff-finding-classification-architecture-2026-11-06.md`, kept outside the repository.
- **The first agreement check reported its own failure.** [ST-0390](../../backlog/ST-0390.md) changed no rulebook to pass, and the [report](finding-classification-agreement-check-2026-11-07.md) says "The check failed." The stakeholder then decided the next step.
- **Running the code found what reading did not.** The [Fagan inspection](fagan-review-2026-11-08-finding-classification.md) found no blocking defect, and the [bug hunt](bug-hunt-2026-11-08-finding-classification.md) found [BUG-0123](../findings/BUG-0123.md) and [BUG-0124](../findings/BUG-0124.md).
- **The migration gave real answers about old defects.** The damage checks replayed 74 story merges in six ledgers for BUG-0086 and found no lost line. Evidence: [the migration report](migration-2026-11-08-finding-classification.md) and the notebook.
- **The blocking rule was useful before its machinery existed.** Reviewers applied "only `defect` or `risk` at `critical` or `major` blocks" from the proposal text alone. Every review of the counter builds closed after at most four passes. Evidence: [retro-2026-11-06.md](retro-2026-11-06.md).

## 🛑 Stop Doing

- **Accepting a proposal whose stated problem has no measurement.** A build that exists to reduce a number needs that number first. Evidence: the variance claim of finding-classification, measured only after the build started.
- **Treating a failed gate as a reason to change the gate's inputs without asking whether the design is needed.** The first check failed, and the answer was a sharper rulebook on a fresh set. The stakeholder's choice was valid, but nobody put the question "is the old rule good enough?" to the stakeholder. Evidence: [the first check](finding-classification-agreement-check-2026-11-07.md) and [the second check](finding-classification-agreement-check-2-2026-11-08.md).
- **Stating a distribution without counting it.** Evidence: the "nearly all `major`" claim in the first agreement report and the notebook.
- **Pinning a derived count in a test.** This is the second occurrence. Evidence: the chapter 5 counts of 54 and 57.
- **Using text matching as a security boundary.** This is the second occurrence. Evidence: the guardrail hook (T-0017) and `guard-human-fields`.

## ▶️ Continue Doing

- **Running a small experiment before committing to a mechanism** — it replaces argument with a result. Evidence: the race test and the union-merge test.
- **Running the code in a separate QA pass** — reading alone missed both major defects. Evidence: the bug hunt of 2026-11-08.
- **Taking every ID from `next-id`** — the reports of both finding-classification and counter-ids-fitting record no duplicate number. Evidence: the notebook's standing instructions and [retro-2026-11-06.md](retro-2026-11-06.md).
- **Keeping a notebook through a long automated build** — it survived a restart and several compactions. Evidence: the [implementation notebook](../spec/work/finding-classification-implementation-notebook.md).
