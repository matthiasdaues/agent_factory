# Classify a finding with the one rulebook

**Priority:** must-have

## Goal

A reviewer agent reads one rulebook, `finding-classification.md`, and gets the severity of any finding from the answers it records. The rulebook holds the scale, the blocking rule, and one rule per review class, each with calibration examples. No other rulebook defines a severity level.

## Domain Rule

- A finding has four values: category, type, severity, and blocking. The reviewer sets category and type; the rule of the class computes the severity; category and severity give blocking (BR-085).
- The reviewed material decides the class, for a review and for any other source. Playbooks, skills, agent definitions, rulebooks, and hook configuration are code (BR-079, DEC-17, DEC-37).
- The consequence tree asks `harms_downstream`, `fails_purpose`, and `raises_cost` in that order; the first yes gives `critical`, `major`, or `minor`, and three no answers give `note` (BR-081).
- The bug bar starts at the level of the effect class and lowers one step per true precondition (BR-082). The matrix gives the severity of an architecture risk from importance and exposure (BR-083).
- A `suggestion` is at most `minor` (BR-084). A `question` becomes a todo entry only.
- An attack that needs the capability it gains is not a finding.
- A waiver keeps the computed severity. A `critical` finding is waived or resolved only after its stop is confirmed (DEC-47, DEC-52).
- Each class section lists two or three real findings per level with their basis. A level without a real finding has one invented example that names real project files.

## Outputs

- `packages/factory/rulebooks/conventions/finding-classification.md`: the values of a finding, the shared scale and the blocking rule, in use, the consequence tree, the type from the fix, the sections `Class: document`, `Class: code`, `Class: security`, `Class: architecture`, and `Class: runtime`, the waiver and confirmation rules, and the calibration examples.
- `report-format.md` § Category rules and § Severity, `finding-format.md` § When to file, `owasp-top-10.md`, and the filing rule in `rules.md` link the rulebook and define no level.
- The envelope example in `report-format.md` carries `critical`, `major`, `minor`, and `note`.

## Boundaries

- The rulebook takes its rules from BR-079 to BR-085 and its wording from the proposal's § Design. A case those sources leave open is a stop condition, not a new rule.
- Calibration examples are real findings with their basis. FAGAN-0021, SEC-0005, and SEC-0006 are never examples, because the agreement check uses them (proposal § Agreement check). ST-0390 picks its other 17 findings around the examples.
- `adversarial-review` keeps its own scale (DEC-36). The rulebook names only its envelope counts.
- No file outside the rulebook keeps a severity definition. A link replaces it.

## Agent Stop Conditions

- A level of a class has neither a real finding nor a project file an invented example could name. Report the class and the level.
- A real finding fits two classes under BR-079. Report the finding and both classes; do not pick one.
- The rulebook text and BR-079 to BR-085 disagree on a severity. Report the conflicting files and the decision needed; do not add a workaround.
