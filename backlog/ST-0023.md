# Author role-separation and claim-admission policies

Write the two enforceable core policies as prose rulebooks under the new
`factory/rulebooks/conventions/research-` category. These documents state the
rules that the policy validator and the playbook must obey; they do not
implement the checks themselves.

The role-separation policy states that a claim author cannot review or vote on
that claim, a reviewer cannot edit the claim, the orchestrator cannot vote,
the report writer cannot create findings, and one agent cannot fill
conflicting roles for the same claim. The claim-admission policy states the
full set of conditions a claim must meet to enter the final report: one
assertion, clear scope, explicit assumptions, a stated refutation condition,
required tests run, evidence checks passed, required reviews completed, a
strict majority of decisive votes for survival, no blocking defect, no
unanswered material refutation, and all votes referencing the current claim
version. It records that a vote decides whether a claim met the process
standard, not whether it is true.

**Priority:** must-have

## Acceptance Criteria

- `role-separation.md` states all five role-conflict rules from the proposal's
  Role-Separation Policy.
- `claim-admission-policy.md` states all admission conditions from the
  proposal's Claim-Admission Policy, including strict-majority-of-decisive-votes
  and the current-claim-version requirement.
- Both documents carry frontmatter that `index-lint` can read, so they are
  discovered under the `policies` category.
- Each policy names the artifacts and roles it governs, so a reader can trace a
  rule to the fields that carry it.
- The documents describe rules only; they contain no schema definitions and no
  step ordering.
