# Re-measure the agreement on the severity after sharpening two class rules

**Priority:** must-have

## Goal

The stakeholder reads a second agreement report in `docs/reviews/`. It shows how often two runs give the same severity to 20 fresh findings under the sharpened rulebook, and how often under the old rules. The check passes under the same condition as before: the new runs agree on at least 16 of 20 and on more findings than the old runs.

## Domain Rule

- The first check failed: 18 of 20 under the new rulebook, 20 of 20 under the old rules. The two new runs split on SPEC-0012 (document) and BUG-0006 (runtime), `major` against `minor`. The old runs agreed because they rated nearly every finding `major` (report of 2026-11-07).
- Part 1 sharpens the line between `major` and `minor` for the document and runtime classes. It adds no rule. The scale, the consequence tree, and every BR stay as they are.
- Part 2 measures with the sharpened rulebook. The rulebook is committed before the measurement starts and does not change after it.
- The fresh set shares no finding with the first report. It holds no calibration example of the rulebook, no worked example of the proposal, and nothing the proposal, the rulebook, or the feature file names.
- The quotas, the fresh subagents, the model, the four runs, and the rule that a `question` counts as disagreement are those of ST-0390 (DEC-39).
- The pass condition is DEC-23. A second failure goes to the stakeholder; the story does not change the rulebook to pass.

## Outputs

- `finding-classification.md` with a sharper `major`/`minor` line for the document and runtime classes, in one commit made before the measurement.
- One report in `docs/reviews/` with the cause of each split, the sharpened text, the quotas, the 20 picks, the model, the new count, the old count, and whether the check passed.

## Demo Scenario

1. The orchestrator reads the first report and the two findings. It states for SPEC-0012 and BUG-0006 which answer to `fails_purpose` each run gave and why the two answers differ.
2. It edits the document and runtime class sections of `finding-classification.md` and adds one calibration example where it helps. The rulebook commit lands.
3. It picks 20 fresh findings by the quotas and writes their reduced texts. It starts four fresh subagents: two with the sharpened rulebook, two with the old `report-format.md` from `git show 74e036f1:packages/factory/rulebooks/conventions/report-format.md`.
4. The stakeholder opens `docs/reviews/finding-classification-agreement-check-2-<date>.md`. It reads, for example, "new rules: 17 of 20 agree; old rules: 19 of 20 agree; not passed" or "new rules: 18 of 20 agree; old rules: 14 of 20 agree; passed", beside the first report's 18 and 20.

## Demo Data

None. The 20 findings are real closed files of `docs/findings/`.

## Boundaries

- Part 1 precedes Part 2. The rulebook commit exists before the first run starts, and no commit edits the rulebook after it.
- No change to the shared scale, the consequence tree, the `in_use` rule, a `critical` rule, or any BR. A change of that kind stops the story and goes to the stakeholder.
- Each sharpening is a question whose answer decides the line, written in the form of the existing class tables, plus at most one added calibration example per class. A calibration example is real or invented, as `finding-classification.md` states for all of them.
- The added calibration example is not a finding of the fresh set. Neither SPEC-0012 nor BUG-0006 becomes an example, because both stay measured in the first report only.
- No run gets the conversation, the proposal, the first report, or any other file than the rules under test and the 20 texts.
- The report states a failed check in the same plain words as a passed one. It records both counts of both checks.
- The check runs once by hand; no script is written (deferred, as in ST-0390).
- No test reads the report (QA strategy § One-time checks).

## Out of Scope

- A script for the check.
- A change of the scale, the consequence tree, a BR, or a class other than document and runtime.
- A third measurement. A second failure stops the story.
- Any rulebook change after the measurement.

## Agent Stop Conditions

- The sharpening needs a change of the scale, the consequence tree, or a BR. Report the rule and the decision needed; do not edit it.
- The sharpening cannot be written as a deciding question plus one example. Report the two readings and stop.
- `docs/findings/` holds fewer eligible closed findings of a class than its quota once the first set, the calibration examples, and the named examples are left out. Report the class and the count.
- The check fails. Report both counts, both reports, and the split findings to the stakeholder and stop.
- The rulebook and the first report disagree on a class rule. Report the lines and the decision needed; do not add a workaround.

## Decisions of 2026-11-08

- The first agreement check failed (18 of 20 new, 20 of 20 old). The stakeholder chose to sharpen the rulebook where the two new runs split, then measure again on a fresh set. The alternatives, to accept the result or to change the pass condition, were not chosen.
- The order is binding: the rulebook change is committed before the measurement starts, and the rulebook does not change after it.
- The fresh set keeps 20 findings and the target of 16, so the second count compares with the first (stakeholder, 2026-11-08). No fresh security finding is eligible, so the three security slots go to the document and runtime classes, which the sharpening targets: the quotas become 8 document, 5 code, 5 runtime, 2 architecture, 0 security. The report states this deviation from DEC-39.
