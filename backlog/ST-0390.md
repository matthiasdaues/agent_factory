# Measure how often two runs agree on the severity

**Priority:** must-have

## Goal

The stakeholder reads one review report in `docs/reviews/` that shows how often two runs give the same severity to 20 fixed findings under the new rulebook, and how often under the old rules. The check passes when the new runs agree on at least 16 of 20 and on more findings than the old runs.

## Domain Rule

- The set holds 20 closed findings from `docs/findings/`, none of them migrated: 6 document, 5 code, 4 runtime, 2 architecture, and 3 security (FAGAN-0021, SEC-0005, SEC-0006).
- No finding of the set is a calibration example of the rulebook or a worked example of the proposal.
- Each finding keeps only the text that says what is wrong and how it was fixed.
- Each of the four runs is a fresh subagent session that gets only the rules under test and the 20 texts. All four use the model of the review agents.
- Two runs use `finding-classification.md`; two use `report-format.md` as it stood before ST-0386.
- A `question` counts as disagreement.

## Outputs

- One review report in `docs/reviews/` with the quotas, the 20 picks, the model, the new agreement count, the old agreement count, and whether the check passed.

## Demo Scenario

1. The orchestrator picks the 20 findings and writes their reduced texts.
2. It starts four fresh subagents, two with `finding-classification.md` and two with the old `report-format.md` from `git show 74e036f1:packages/factory/rulebooks/conventions/report-format.md`.
3. The stakeholder opens the report in `docs/reviews/`. It reads, for example, "new rules: 17 of 20 agree; old rules: 11 of 20 agree; passed".

## Demo Data

None. The 20 findings are real closed files of `docs/findings/`.

## Boundaries

- The check runs once by hand; no script is written (deferred).
- No run gets the conversation, the proposal, or any other file.
- The report states a failed check in the same plain words as a passed one. A failed check is reported to the stakeholder; the story does not change the rulebook to pass.
- No test reads the report (QA strategy § One-time checks).
